- The HUD's layout, proportions, typography and styling.

Preserve each screen's individual identity. Screen 04's gold tunnel, Screen 06's curling crest, Screen 07's winding river and Screen 08's final chamber should each remain recognisable.

## 2. Maintain the approved appearance throughout movement

The opening frame is only one part of the requirement.

The approved visual quality must continue through the entrance, early movement, middle, late section, ending, left steering, right steering and transition into the next screen.

I expect perspective to change naturally as Benny moves. Nearby objects should pass the camera, distant objects should approach, and scenery should become visible from new angles. Throughout that movement, the world must retain the approved design, materials, colours, proportions and quality.

When movement reveals a surface or area that the original picture did not show, make it a convincing continuation of that same approved environment.

- Approaches and passes a gold ring.

- Enters the next environment.

Those moving views are part of the design requirement. They need the same care as the opening view.

## 3. Make the water clean, detailed and consistent with each reference

The water is one of the most important parts of this game. It occupies much of the screen and needs to remain attractive while moving.

Match each approved screen's water in colour, shape, gloss, depth and detail:

- Preserve the bright cyan and blue body of the river, with the appropriate pink or magenta edges.
- Match the approved balance of blue, pink, white highlights and reflected colour.
- Keep highlights shaped and sized appropriately. Large white sheets, excessively long glints and sharp spikes should not overwhelm the water.
- Give the water coherent curves, ripples and flowing detail that follow the river.
- Keep the surface visually continuous around Benny and between sections of the channel.
- Resolve visible seams, speckled joins, stretched textures, cut-out edges, abrupt colour changes and patches that look detached from the surrounding water.
- Keep the pink edges fluid and glossy, with the height and thickness appropriate to that screen.
- Make reflections and highlights belong to the water's surface and movement.

Match the foam to the original reference too. Where it forms soft banks, it should have rounded, varied shapes and believable spacing. Where the reference shows individual bubbles, preserve that distinction. Avoid making every screen use the same foam treatment.

Benny's contact with the water should feel natural. Ripples, splashes and his wake should join the surrounding surface cleanly and respond to his movement.

## 4. Keep the scenery detailed and the camera useful during play

The background and surrounding scenery matter just as much as Benny.

As he passes through the environment:

- Gold rings should retain their approved shape, spacing, metallic appearance and lighting.
- Perforated washer walls should retain clear, properly shaped details as they approach the camera.
- Curved surfaces should remain visually coherent through turns and changes in perspective.
- The scene should preserve its approved sense of scale and depth.
- Lighting and colour should remain consistent across neighbouring surfaces and stage connections.

The camera should follow Benny naturally and keep the upcoming route understandable. Steering should allow me to see where he is going while preserving the intended feeling of swimming through the washer.

Please correct any view where a nearby wall or oversized foreground surface takes over so much of the screen that the approved river and scenery disappear.

## 5. Keep Benny consistent with his approved appearance

Use the approved Benny throughout the game:

- The same fluffy pink body, silhouette and proportions.
- The same purple-and-cream knitted hat, with the correct stripe pattern, width and texture.
- The same pom-pom and yellow star, with the correct shape, scale and placement.
- The same soft, detailed fur and overall character quality.

His hat should retain its knitted appearance, and his body should retain its soft, fluffy appearance at normal phone viewing size.

Keep him at the correct waterline and scale for the camera. His swimming, leaning and turning should feel connected to the river. His wake and splashes should follow his movement.

Allow natural perspective changes and animation while preserving his identity. The character should look consistent when centred, steering left, steering right, approaching the camera or moving farther away.
